## Line 3 — Each face is machined differently (Frame 3)

**Time:** 1:39–4:30

    [calm] Now we look at how the part is made. [calm] There is one principle here. [cautionary] **You do not cut every face the same way.** What touches a face decides how it is machined.

    (1 row — bore Ø25 H7) [pointing] Start with the hole in the middle. [measured] Diameter 25, grade H7. [calm] First you drill it at 24. [calm] Then you finish it with a reamer. [calm] The surface finish is fine, Ra 1.6.

    [asking] Why is only this one finished fine? [calm] Because a turning shaft sits here. [calm] A rough surface lets shaft and bore scrape each other. [calm] Friction rises, heat comes with it, and both wear.

    [calm] Once they wear, the fit you set at the start goes loose. [calm] That is to say, the shaft starts to move about. [calm] Finished fine, friction drops, the shaft turns smoothly and the part holds its life. [calm] Grade H7 cannot be held by a drill alone, so a reaming operation goes in on top.

    (2 row — tapped 4-M5, 10 deep) [pointing] These are the four small holes around the bore. [calm] They are the screw seats that hold the shaft cover. [calm] You drill them at 4.2. [calm] Then you cut the thread with a tap. [calm] No surface finish is specified here.

    [calm] What matters instead is the drill diameter. [emphatic] Too large and only a shallow thread remains. [calm] Then the screw cannot take its load. [calm] There is a reason the four are evenly spaced, too — so the cover does not tilt to one side.

    (3 row — mounting slots 2-R5) [pointing] These are the two long holes at the bottom. [calm] You drill twice at diameter 10. [calm] Then you join them with an end mill. [measured] Rough is fine here, Ra 12.5.

    [asking] Why not finish these finely? [emphatic] Because the bolt only passes through. [calm] The bolt head and the frame clamp the bracket between them. [calm] It does not take load against the wall of the hole. [calm] Smooth or rough, the result is the same.

    [calm] And finishing it finely adds an operation. [calm] Finish where it is not needed and all you buy is cost. [calm] That judgement shows up directly in the machine shop's quote.

    (4 row — boss front face Ø56) [pointing] This is the round part at the top. [calm] It is cut by step milling. [calm] The finish is medium, Ra 6.3. [calm] The cover lands on this face, so flat is enough. [calm] It does not have to be fine.

    [asking] Why does this part stand forward? [calm] The whole plate could have been made thicker. [emphatic] Instead only the material around the shaft, where it is needed, is raised 8 forward.

    (5 row — web and outer profile) [pointing] This is the outer profile of the plate and the web rising through the middle. [calm] Profile milling gives it its shape and nothing more. [calm] Rough is fine, because these faces touch no other part. [emphatic] They only carry load.

    (6 row — fillet R10) [pointing] This is the inside corner where the web turns into the plate. [calm] It comes from the corner radius of the end mill. [asking] Why round it? [calm] Meet it as a sharp corner and load piles onto that one point. [calm] That is where the crack starts.

    [calm] A radius spreads the load out. [calm] This is where the load is greatest on this part. [calm] That is why the R is as large as 10.

    [calm] A real drawing carries more notation than this — surface finish symbols, geometric tolerance, heat treatment. [cautionary] You do not draw those in this course. [emphatic] We will only learn what each symbol asks for, and move on.

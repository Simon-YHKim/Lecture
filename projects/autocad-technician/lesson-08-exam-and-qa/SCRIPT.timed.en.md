## Line 3 — Five questions that keep coming up (Frame 3)

**Time:** 1:52–3:33

    `[1:51–1:57]` Five questions came up again and again in review. Here is an answer to each.

    `[1:57–2:16]` (1 card) First, what are the criteria? This is the question we hear most. The marking scheme and the pass mark are to be shared. When the announcement lands, check the weightings and the deductions. The cross-checks in this course are practice — they help you find your own mistakes.

    `[2:16–2:27]` (2 card) Second, who do I ask when I get stuck? Ask Kim Jeong-ung or Kim Yang-hwan. Do not sit with it alone. The places people get stuck are usually the same ones.

    `[2:28–2:47]` (3 card) Third, how do I get good at projection? The best way is to do some 3D modelling yourself. Turning a shape around teaches your hands where each face goes. If that is not possible, draw a lot of 2D drawings. Looking again at the drawing you made in this course counts too.

    `[2:48–3:02]` (4 card) Fourth, what do I do when a hidden line and a visible line fall on the same place? Show it as the visible line. The line you can see wins. Drawing both on top of each other only confuses the reader.

    `[3:03–3:27]` (5 card) Fifth, how far out do I place the dimension lines? There is no exact rule. When it looks tidy, it is right. That is hard to judge, so keep a well-made drawing beside you. And take as many dimensions as you can from the visible outline of the front view. Only what the front view cannot show goes to the side and top views.

## Line 4 — What a technician actually does with this (Frame 4)

**Time:** 3:33–4:57

    `[3:30–3:40]` After the LG Innotek internal exam you use this on the floor. Here are three ways it serves equipment PM and preventive maintenance.

    `[3:41–4:04]` (1 card) First, proposing a jig. Holding the same work by hand every time takes longer and comes out differently for each person. You draw the jig that would make the job easier and propose it. Asking in words and handing over a drawing are not the same thing. The drawing carries dimensions. The person making it does not have to come back and ask.

    `[4:04–4:27]` (2 card) Second, checking a layout. You check where equipment will stand on a drawing first. You look at whether a walkway is left and whether the door still opens. You check for clashes with the machine next to it. A problem found on a drawing can be fixed. What happens if you find it after the machine arrives? By then you cannot move it.

    `[4:28–4:50]` (3 card) Third, sourcing a spare part in a hurry. A machine is down and you need one part fast. First you find the part in the assembly drawing. Then you check the spare part drawing and talk to the machine shop. If you cannot read the drawing, this stops here. Reading and drawing exist so that this conversation is exact.

## Line 5 — Commands you reach for on the floor (Frame 5)

**Time:** 4:57–6:45

    `[4:53–5:03]` There are six commands in the table on screen. Five of them you already typed in the drawing lessons. The same command lands in a different place on the floor.

    `[5:04–5:24]` (1 row) Polyline, `PL`. It draws connected segments as one object. Whether to keep separate lines or bind them into a polyline follows the editing you plan. On a layout you run one around the machine footprint. Then the area comes out in one go, and it follows along when you move it.

    `[5:24–5:35]` (2 row) Offset, `O`. It makes the same shape a set distance away. You get wall thickness and walkway clearance without picking a centre again.

    `[5:35–5:46]` (3 row) Distance, `DI`. It measures between two points. You check on the spot whether the machine will fit. It leaves nothing behind on the drawing.

    `[5:46–6:01]` (4 row) List, `LI`. It shows what a selected object is. On a drawing you received you check whether that line really is the length it looks. Two lines can look the same and hold different values.

    `[6:01–6:15]` (5 row) Match properties, `MA`. It carries properties over to another object. Drawings from other people come with mixed layers. You send them back where they belong without redrawing.

    `[6:15–6:29]` (6 row) Plot, `PLOT`. It sends the drawing to paper or PDF. When you talk to the machine shop you do not show them a screen. You hand them paper. You check the scale before it goes out.

    `[6:29–6:36]` You do not have to memorise the six today. Remember the situations. Stand in that spot and the command comes back.

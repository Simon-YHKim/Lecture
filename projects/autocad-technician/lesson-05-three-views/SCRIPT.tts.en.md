## Line 3 — The three views hold each other in place (Frame 3)

**Time:** 1:23–3:36

    [calm] Before you draw, four things to look at.
    [cautionary] You can draw every line accurately and still put a view in the wrong place.

    (1 card — settle the front view first) [pointing] Three views start from the front view.
    [calm] There are tests for what you take as the front view.
    [calm] First, the face that shows the shape best.
    [calm] For this part, the face where the base, the web and the boss are all visible at once.
    [calm] Second, the attitude the part actually sits or assembles in.
    [calm] This part bolts to a vertical frame face with two bolts. [calm] You look at it in that attitude.
    [calm] Third, the direction that yields the fewest hidden lines.
    [calm] In the LG Innotek internal exam you are given a three-dimensional picture. [calm] This is the thing to settle first.
    [calm] Once it is settled, the other two views follow.
    [calm] Third angle puts a view on the side you looked from.
    [calm] Looked at from above, it goes above.
    [calm] Looked at from the right, it goes to the right.
    [calm] So above the front view is the top view.
    [calm] To the right of the front view is the right side view.
    [calm] First angle places them the other way round.
    [calm] What was seen from above goes below.
    [cautionary] The same part laid out differently, and the reader takes up and down the wrong way round.
    [calm] The equipment assembly drawings you get on the floor are mostly third angle too.
    [calm] When you check a spare part drawing in a hurry, you have to know this layout.
    [calm] Our drawing is third angle as well.

    (2 card — projection lines) [cautionary] They exist so that you never measure the same value twice.
    [measured] The front view's width of 120 is the top view's width unchanged.
    [calm] The front view's height of 90 is the right side view's height unchanged.
    [calm] Line them up by eye and they drift a little.
    [calm] That drift shows up later, when you dimension.
    [calm] Hang a projection line and it cannot drift.
    [calm] You are lashing the views to each other.

    (3 card — hidden lines) [pointing] You draw edges that material hides from view.
    [calm] The judgement has three branches.
    [calm] Visible, and it is a visible line.
    [calm] There but not visible, and it is a hidden line.
    [cautionary] Not there at all, and you do not draw it.
    [calm] The bore goes right through, front to back.
    [calm] But look down from above and material covers it.
    [calm] So in the top view it is dashed.

    (4 card — joining with a 45 degree line) [emphatic] This is the part people find most confusing today.
    [calm] Depth is not in the front view.
    [calm] It is settled for the first time in the top view.
    [calm] The device that carries that depth into the right side view is the 45 degree line.
    [calm] A line running right out of the top view turns at the 45 degree line.
    [calm] It turns and comes down.
    [calm] A vertical distance in the top view becomes a horizontal distance in the right side view.
    [calm] Because it is 45 degrees, the two distances are equal.
    [cautionary] Measure it with a rule and carry it over and you will be wrong; carry it on this line and you cannot be.

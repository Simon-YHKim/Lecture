## Line 2 — A drawing speaks for you (Frame 2)

**Time:** 0:11–1:49

    [calm] Let us start with why you learn drawings at all.

    [calm] Standing in front of a machine, you have probably said something like this. [calm] "Just shave off about this much here."

    [calm] To the person saying it, that can feel like a clear instruction. [asking] Did the person listening understand the same thing?

    [calm] It is hard to tell which part to cut, and by how much.

    [calm] That is when you open a drawing and talk over it. [asking] What has to be written on it?

    (1 card) [pointing] First, shape, size and form. [calm] It shows what the part looks like and what its dimensions are.

    (2 card) [light] Second, orientation and position. [calm] You check where a feature sits and which way it faces. [calm] The same hole at a different distance from the datum face makes a different part.

    (3 card) [pointing] Third, surface condition, material and machining. [calm] You write down what it is made of and how it is made.

    (4 card) [pointing] Fourth, the meaning has to be unambiguous. [calm] If two readers interpret it differently, the parts they make can differ too.

    (5 card) [pointing] Fifth, a stored drawing has to be findable and reusable. [calm] You leave it so that years later someone can pull it out, read it and use it.

    (6 below) [asking] Why does a technician need this?

    [calm] Think about the work you do on equipment yourself.

    [calm] When a task is awkward, you draw a jig and propose an improvement.

    [calm] You also check on a drawing first where new equipment will stand. [calm] You find blocked walkways or clashes with nearby machines before they happen.

    [calm] When a machine stops and you need a spare part quickly, you need a drawing as well. [calm] You find the part in the assembly drawing and check its part drawing. [calm] Then you talk to the machine shop with that drawing in front of you both.

    [calm] That is why you learn drawings. [emphatic] It is to pass on the intent of the work exactly. [cautionary] It also cuts the loss of re-machining a part that came out wrong.

## Line 3 — What the practice tasks look like (Frame 3)

**Time:** 1:49–2:51

    [calm] In this course you draw these yourself, so that you can do that work. [calm] The two formats introduced here are practice examples for this course. [calm] The exam's task format and how it is run are confirmed in the announcement that will be shared.

    (1 card) [pointing] A practice example gives you the shape and the dimensions. [calm] Sometimes as a three-dimensional picture, sometimes as a piece of a drawing with dimensions on it.

    (2 card) [pointing] There are two practice formats. [emphatic] The first is drawing the shape exactly to the dimensions you are given.

    (3 card) [pointing] The other is orthographic projection. [calm] In our practice you look at the view from one direction and draw the others. [calm] The front view, top view and right side view have to agree with each other.

    (4 card) [pointing] The line types are checked as well. [calm] Visible line, centerline, hidden line and dimension line each go on their own layer.

    [cautionary] Even when the shape is right, mixing up what each line means can make the drawing read wrong.

    (5 below) [pointing] One thing to note in passing.

    [calm] Sheet size, layer names and colors can differ from task to task. [calm] The values this course sets are practice defaults. [calm] If the task sheet in front of you states something else, follow the task sheet.

## Line 4 — One part, drawn to the end, and the exam (Frame 4)

**Time:** 2:51–4:35

    [calm] Now let us see what you learn, and in what order.

    [calm] In this course you finish one part across six lessons. [calm] It is the bracket that supports the idler pulley on a car engine.

    [measured] Practice runs on AutoCAD 2024. [calm] No starter file is handed out. [calm] You create a new drawing yourself in Lesson 2, and from the next lesson on you open the file you saved and carry on. [calm] At the end of a lesson, save it under the name given. [calm] It is the starting point for the next one.

    [calm] When you practise again you can pick up from the point you saved.

    (1 row) [pointing] Lesson 1 is the orientation you are watching now, and the course proper starts at Lesson 2.

    (2 row) [pointing] In Lesson 2 you read what this part is and why it has this shape. [calm] Then you build a border and layers on an A3 sheet and keep it as a template.

    (3 row) [pointing] In Lesson 3 you draw the outer shape of the base and the web. [calm] You catch points with object snaps, set the direction with the cursor, then type the length. [emphatic] It is practice at fixing the points and lengths you need, exactly.

    (4 row) [pointing] In Lesson 4 you fill in the inner features with circles and arcs. [calm] The bore, the tapped holes, the slots and the fillets at the web. [calm] The tapped holes repeat with an array. [calm] The offset you learn here also serves for wall thickness or for clearance in a walkway.

    (5 row) [pointing] In Lesson 5 you draw the top view and the right side view in third-angle projection. [calm] You carry height and width across from the front view. [calm] Add the depth and the three views line up.

    (6 row) [pointing] In Lesson 6 you clear away the construction lines that are left. [calm] Then you read the symbols that appear on a real drawing.

    (7 row) [pointing] In Lesson 7 you put the dimensions on, check the scale and export. [calm] The part drawing you have carried since Lesson 2 is finished here.

    (8 row) [emphatic] In the last lesson, Lesson 8, you work through the questions that come up most and list what to check in the exam announcement. [calm] The marking scheme, the pass mark and how the exam is run are to be shared.

## Line 5 — This lesson and the next (Frame 5)

**Time:** 4:35–5:20

    (1 left) [light] Four things from today.

    [calm] A drawing carries the author's intent so that anyone reading it takes the same meaning. [calm] You saw the five things a drawing has to have.

    [calm] You also learned to tell the two practice formats apart: drawing to given dimensions, and orthographic projection. [calm] From Lesson 2 you carry one saved file forward and finish one part.

    (2 right) [pointing] The course proper starts next time.

    [calm] In Lesson 2 you look at what the idler pulley bracket does. [calm] You also learn how each face is machined, and why that method was chosen.

    [asking] What does the machining method do to the life and the performance of the part? [calm] You read the notes on the drawing and connect the two.

    [calm] After that you build a border and a title block on an A3 sheet. [calm] You set up four layers and leave a template file.

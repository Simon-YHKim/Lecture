## Line 2 — A drawing speaks for you (Frame 2)

**Time:** 0:11–1:49

    Let us start with why you learn drawings at all.

    Standing in front of a machine, you have probably said something like this. "Just shave off about this much here."

    To the person saying it, that can feel like a clear instruction. Did the person listening understand the same thing?

    It is hard to tell which part to cut, and by how much.

    That is when you open a drawing and talk over it. What has to be written on it?

    (1 card) First, shape, size and form. It shows what the part looks like and what its dimensions are.

    (2 card) Second, orientation and position. You check where a feature sits and which way it faces. The same hole at a different distance from the datum face makes a different part.

    (3 card) Third, surface condition, material and machining. You write down what it is made of and how it is made.

    (4 card) Fourth, the meaning has to be unambiguous. If two readers interpret it differently, the parts they make can differ too.

    (5 card) Fifth, a stored drawing has to be findable and reusable. You leave it so that years later someone can pull it out, read it and use it.

    (6 below) Why does a technician need this?

    Think about the work you do on equipment yourself.

    When a task is awkward, you draw a jig and propose an improvement.

    You also check on a drawing first where new equipment will stand. You find blocked walkways or clashes with nearby machines before they happen.

    When a machine stops and you need a spare part quickly, you need a drawing as well. You find the part in the assembly drawing and check its part drawing. Then you talk to the machine shop with that drawing in front of you both.

    That is why you learn drawings. It is to pass on the intent of the work exactly. It also cuts the loss of re-machining a part that came out wrong.

## Line 3 — What the practice tasks look like (Frame 3)

**Time:** 1:49–2:51

    In this course you draw these yourself, so that you can do that work. The two formats introduced here are practice examples for this course. The exam's task format and how it is run are confirmed in the announcement that will be shared.

    (1 card) A practice example gives you the shape and the dimensions. Sometimes as a three-dimensional picture, sometimes as a piece of a drawing with dimensions on it.

    (2 card) There are two practice formats. The first is drawing the shape exactly to the dimensions you are given.

    (3 card) The other is orthographic projection. In our practice you look at the view from one direction and draw the others. The front view, top view and right side view have to agree with each other.

    (4 card) The line types are checked as well. Visible line, centerline, hidden line and dimension line each go on their own layer.

    Even when the shape is right, mixing up what each line means can make the drawing read wrong.

    (5 below) One thing to note in passing.

    Sheet size, layer names and colors can differ from task to task. The values this course sets are practice defaults. If the task sheet in front of you states something else, follow the task sheet.

## Line 4 — One part, drawn to the end, and the exam (Frame 4)

**Time:** 2:51–4:35

    Now let us see what you learn, and in what order.

    In this course you finish one part across six lessons. It is the bracket that supports the idler pulley on a car engine.

    Practice runs on AutoCAD 2024. No starter file is handed out. You create a new drawing yourself in Lesson 2, and from the next lesson on you open the file you saved and carry on. At the end of a lesson, save it under the name given. It is the starting point for the next one.

    When you practise again you can pick up from the point you saved.

    (1 row) Lesson 1 is the orientation you are watching now, and the course proper starts at Lesson 2.

    (2 row) In Lesson 2 you read what this part is and why it has this shape. Then you build a border and layers on an A3 sheet and keep it as a template.

    (3 row) In Lesson 3 you draw the outer shape of the base and the web. You catch points with object snaps, set the direction with the cursor, then type the length. It is practice at fixing the points and lengths you need, exactly.

    (4 row) In Lesson 4 you fill in the inner features with circles and arcs. The bore, the tapped holes, the slots and the fillets at the web. The tapped holes repeat with an array. The offset you learn here also serves for wall thickness or for clearance in a walkway.

    (5 row) In Lesson 5 you draw the top view and the right side view in third-angle projection. You carry height and width across from the front view. Add the depth and the three views line up.

    (6 row) In Lesson 6 you clear away the construction lines that are left. Then you read the symbols that appear on a real drawing.

    (7 row) In Lesson 7 you put the dimensions on, check the scale and export. The part drawing you have carried since Lesson 2 is finished here.

    (8 row) In the last lesson, Lesson 8, you work through the questions that come up most and list what to check in the exam announcement. The marking scheme, the pass mark and how the exam is run are to be shared.

## Line 5 — This lesson and the next (Frame 5)

**Time:** 4:35–5:20

    (1 left) Four things from today.

    A drawing carries the author's intent so that anyone reading it takes the same meaning. You saw the five things a drawing has to have.

    You also learned to tell the two practice formats apart: drawing to given dimensions, and orthographic projection. From Lesson 2 you carry one saved file forward and finish one part.

    (2 right) The course proper starts next time.

    In Lesson 2 you look at what the idler pulley bracket does. You also learn how each face is machined, and why that method was chosen.

    What does the machining method do to the life and the performance of the part? You read the notes on the drawing and connect the two.

    After that you build a border and a title block on an A3 sheet. You set up four layers and leave a template file.
